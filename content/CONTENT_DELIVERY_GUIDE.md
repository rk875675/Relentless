# Relentless — Content Delivery Guide for Coach

This document explains exactly how to deliver lesson content so it can be
integrated into the app. Follow this structure for every lesson (Day 1–30)
and the 6 library lessons.

---

## What we need per lesson

Every lesson delivery must include **two things**:

1. A **script document** (PDF, Google Doc, or plain text)
2. **Audio recording files** (WAV preferred)

---

## 1. Script document

Use this template for every lesson script. Copy and fill it in.

```
LESSON TITLE: [short title, e.g. "What MAC Training Actually Is"]
LESSON NUMBER: [e.g. Day 1, Day 14, Library-M-Short]
MAC TAG(S): [one or more of: M, A, C — whichever categories this lesson targets]

--- VOICEOVER ---

[segment_file_name.wav]
[Full spoken script for this recording segment.]
[Write exactly what you'll say — every word.]

[segment_file_name.wav]
[Full spoken script for the next segment.]

--- EXERCISE (if any) ---

[EXERCISE — duration in seconds]
Description of what happens during the pause:
  - What the user should be thinking about or doing
  - Any text prompts that rotate on screen
  - How long each prompt stays

--- JOURNAL PROMPT (if any) ---

[JOURNAL PROMPT]
[The exact question shown to the user.]
```

### Script rules

- **Write every word you'll say.** We use the script to create on-screen
  text that reinforces your voiceover.
- **Mark segment boundaries** with the filename (e.g. `lesson_05_seg_01.wav`).
  Segments are just recording splits — the user never sees them. Split wherever
  is convenient for you. If you nail the whole thing in one take, one segment
  is fine.
- **Mark any exercise pauses** with `[EXERCISE — Xs]` where X is the duration
  in seconds. Describe what should happen during the pause (rotating text
  prompts, breathing cues, etc.). This is where your voiceover stops and
  ambient music plays.
- **Mark any journal prompt** with `[JOURNAL PROMPT]` followed by the exact
  question text.
- **Include the MAC tag(s)** at the top. Just the letter(s): M, A, C, or
  a combination like MA or MAC.

---

## 2. Audio recordings

### File format

- **WAV** (48 kHz, 16-bit, stereo) — preferred
- MP3 or M4A also accepted, but WAV gives us the most flexibility
- Do NOT apply compression, EQ, or effects — deliver dry/clean recordings

### Naming convention

```
lesson_[NN]_seg_[SS].wav
```

| Placeholder | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `NN` | Lesson number, zero-padded | `01`, `14`, `30` |
| `SS` | Segment number, zero-padded | `01`, `02`, `03` |

Examples:
- `lesson_01_seg_01.wav` — Day 1, first segment
- `lesson_01_seg_02.wav` — Day 1, second segment
- `lesson_14_seg_01.wav` — Day 14, only segment (recorded in one take)

For library lessons, use this naming:
```
library_[category]_[length]_seg_[SS].wav
```
Examples:
- `library_mindfulness_short_seg_01.wav`
- `library_acceptance_long_seg_01.wav`

### Segment rules

- Segments are **recording convenience only**. The app plays them back-to-back
  seamlessly. The user never knows where one segment ends and another begins.
- You can split a lesson into as many segments as you want, or record it all
  in one take (one segment).
- If you re-record a section, just re-deliver that segment file. You don't
  need to redo the whole lesson.
- Leave ~0.5s of silence at the start and end of each segment for clean
  stitching.

## Reference: Day 1 delivery (correctly formatted)

```
LESSON TITLE: What MAC Training Actually Is
LESSON NUMBER: Day 1
MAC TAG(S): M

--- VOICEOVER ---

lesson_01_seg_01.wav

Welcome to Relentless.
Before we do anything — I want to explain what you actually signed up
for. Because it's not what most people think.
This is not a meditation app.
This is not positive thinking. It's not visualization boards. It's not
motivational quotes.
This is mental performance training. And there's a difference.

lesson_01_seg_02.wav

The framework behind every lesson in this app is called MAC. Mindfulness.
Acceptance. Commitment. It was introduced in 2001 and it is the most
studied mental performance approach for athletes specifically. Not
soldiers. Not executives. Athletes.

Here's what each one means — in plain terms.

Mindfulness is the ability to notice what your mind is doing right now —
and redirect it. Not clearing your mind. Not thinking about nothing. Just
noticing where your attention is and choosing where it goes.

Acceptance is the willingness to feel uncomfortable — nervous, scared,
doubtful — without treating it like an emergency. You don't eliminate the
feeling. You stop letting it run your decisions.

Commitment is showing up for who you said you are, regardless of how you
feel on any given day. Not motivation. Motivation comes and goes.
Commitment is a decision.

Together, those three things are what every elite performer is doing —
whether they have a name for it or not.

Now you have the name.

Take a second and think about which one you need most.

--- EXERCISE ---

[EXERCISE — 60 seconds]
Ambient music plays. Text cards rotate on screen, auto-advancing
every 20 seconds:
  "M — Mindfulness. Notice where your attention goes. Choose where
   it goes." — 20 seconds
  "A — Acceptance. Feel the discomfort. Don't react to it." — 20 seconds
  "C — Commitment. Show up. Regardless of how you feel." — 20 seconds

--- VOICEOVER (continued) ---

lesson_01_seg_03.wav

Remember your answer.
On Day 30 — I'm going to ask you again.
Journal's on the next screen. One question. Be honest.

--- JOURNAL PROMPT ---

[JOURNAL PROMPT]
Which of the three — Mindfulness, Acceptance, or Commitment — is your
biggest weakness right now? Why?
```
